Germanly

International students and workers in Germany were learning German from fourteen browser tabs. This case study shows how I turned that into one path from A1 to B2 that 120+ people use today.

Learn the word, see the rule, use it

Design Process

Three months, one designer, fourteen learners. Every screen in Germanly traces back to something a learner said, did, or struggled with in front of me.

A1 to B2Mobile first web appAI practicePay once
The problem

Learners in Germany were juggling this…

I moved to Siegen for my HCI Master's and learned German the way everyone around me did: a Duolingo streak, YouTube, PDFs and screenshots. I kept the streak and still froze at the bakery.

…so I designed this.

One product that connects the words people come for with a path that makes them stick, and a clear answer to the question everyone asked: am I ready for the exam?

Who I designed for

Three audiences, one deadline: B1.

Germany now asks for B1 German for citizenship, and skilled workers are expected to reach it. Most people I spoke to had a date in their calendar: a visa appointment, an admission, a residence permit.

    Where Duolingo is better

    Coming back. Streaks, reminders and a game loop keep more people opening the app every day. Germanly lags here, and I say so openly.

    Where Germanly is better

    What sticks. Articles in colour, reviews timed to forgetting, real sentences and readiness toward a real exam. The next goal is to close the retention gap without adding pressure.
